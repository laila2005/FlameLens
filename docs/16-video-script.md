The narration in one block, for reading practice:

> Hi, I'm Laila, from Team AstroSpark. We chose Flame in Freefall, remembering Apollo 1's crew. So we built FlameLens: know the fire evidence before anyone breathes the air. One: enter your cabin's air. Two: FlameLens checks it against eighteen NASA experiments. Three: see what's proven, what's missing, and the closest real test. For habitat engineers and students, FlameLens turns scattered NASA data into clear evidence, for the Moon and Mars. Because every crew deserves to come home.

## Why each part is written this way

- **Apollo 1 is the empathy hook, and it connects to the product.** The Apollo 1 cabin was 100 % oxygen at 16.7 psi. Oxygen and pressure are exactly what FlameLens asks for first, so the opening leads straight into step 1.
- **The Apollo 1 card is text only.** No photos, so there are no image rights to check and nothing graphic. The names are shown, not spoken, to save time.
- **Step 2 is the comparison across PSI experiments.** One design is checked against all 18 PSI investigations at once.
- **Step 3 is the comparison with real NASA reference tests.** On one screen it shows the PSI runs and the cited tests outside PSI (Saffire, drop tower), which are never counted.
- **No safety verdict.** The message is "know the evidence", not "FlameLens makes it safe". "Every crew deserves to come home" is a value, not a claim about the product.

## Recording tips

- Record in a quiet room with a phone held about 20 cm from your mouth. Do 3 takes and keep the best.
- Use a timer: the voice must end by about 0:29, so the end card has a moment of silence.
- If a take runs long, cut these words first: "from" (Team AstroSpark), then "for the Moon and Mars".
- **If Laila's own voice replaces the AI voice, update the AI disclosure** in `docs/FINAL-SUBMISSION.md` and `README.md`: drop the Kokoro narration line. The rules allow adult voices; no one under 18 may appear or be heard.
