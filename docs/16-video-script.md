# 30-second video script (Laila records, Claude edits)

This follows the organizers' outline: 0–2 s introduce, 2–5 s challenge and why, 5–10 s project name and message, 10–20 s how it works and the data, 20–30 s who it helps and future value. The narration has **76 words**, about 2.5 words per second. Speak warmly and clearly. There is no need to rush.

The story: in 1967, the Apollo 1 fire killed three astronauts in a ground test. The cabin was full of pure oxygen and flammable materials. Afterwards NASA's review board said combustible materials "must be severely restricted and controlled". FlameLens carries that lesson forward: before a crew breathes a new cabin's air, the designer should know what NASA's fire experiments actually show.

## What Laila records and sends

1. **The voice.** Read the whole script in one go, 3 times. Leave 1 second of silence before you start. Phone about 20 cm from your mouth, in a quiet room with no fan or AC running. Each take should be 28–29 seconds; time it on a stopwatch.
2. **Optional, for 0:00–0:02:** a short clip of you on camera saying the first line, landscape, eye level, plain background. If you skip this, the team logo goes there.
3. Send the files (the original phone files, not WhatsApp-compressed) or put them in the repo under `media/raw-voice/`. Claude then does the editing: screen captures from the live site, the Apollo 1 cards, captions, music under your voice, and the end card.

If a take runs long, cut these words first: "Moon and Mars" (say "for engineers designing space cabins"), then "Team" in the first line.

## Why it is written this way

- **The 1967 conditions are the same ones FlameLens asks about.** Apollo 1 had 100 % oxygen, 16.7 psi, and combustible materials. FlameLens asks for oxygen, pressure and the material, so the story leads straight into step 1.
- **The Apollo 1 cards are text only.** No photos, so there are no image rights to check and nothing graphic. The crew's names are shown, not spoken, to save time and to be respectful.
- **Step 2 compares across experiments.** One design is checked against all 18 PSI investigations at once.
- **Step 3 compares PSI with real NASA reference tests.** The cited tests outside PSI sit next to the PSI runs, and they are never counted.
- **No safety promise.** The message is "know the evidence", not "FlameLens makes it safe".

**Details from the team's story that we checked and are not using:**
- "Under high pressure": 16.7 psi is only a little above sea level (14.7 psi), so the script says "pure oxygen" and gives the number.
- "3,000-page report", "90 seconds to open the hatch" and "30 miles of wire": not found on the NASA pages above. Use them only if someone finds the NASA source.
- The board's findings do not describe the new outward-opening hatch. It is well known, but it isn't needed in 30 seconds.
